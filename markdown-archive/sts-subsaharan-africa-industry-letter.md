## Assessing Grid Resilience in Extreme Weather

**Objective:** Evaluate North American grid resilience against extreme weather (2021 Texas freeze, 2021 British Columbia heatwave).

**Methodology:** Analyzed historical weather data (NOAA, Environment Canada), NERC reliability standards, industry reports, and conducted stakeholder interviews (grid operators, utilities, regulators). Grid performance modeled using PSS/E software.

**Findings:**

* **Vulnerabilities:**
    - *Generation*: Fossil fuel plants failed due to fuel supply chain disruptions and equipment failures (-40°C in Texas).
    - *Transmission*: Line sagging and equipment failures caused widespread outages (e.g., BC Hydro lost 75% of transmission capacity during the heatwave).

* **Anti-patterns:**
    - **Texas:** Overreliance on natural gas, lack of winterization standards.
    - **British Columbia:** Inadequate reserve margins, insufficient grid hardening against heat.

* **Inefficiencies:**
    - *Communication*: Siloed data sharing hindered coordinated response.
    - *Maintenance*: Reactive maintenance led to equipment failures during extreme events.

**Recommendations:**

a. **Generation:** Diversify fuel sources, implement weatherization standards (winterizing for Texas, heat-resistance for BC).

b. **Transmission:** Upgrade infrastructure with heat-resistant materials and dynamic line ratings technology.

c. **Reserves:** Maintain adequate reserve margins to accommodate unexpected outages.

d. **Data Sharing:** Establish real-time data sharing platforms among stakeholders for enhanced situational awareness.

e. **Maintenance:** Adopt predictive maintenance strategies using machine learning algorithms to anticipate equipment failures.


**Conclusion:** The North American power grid faces significant vulnerabilities to extreme weather, requiring urgent action to improve resilience. Addressing identified anti-patterns and inefficiencies enhances grid reliability while ensuring safety, privacy, and honest communication among stakeholders.

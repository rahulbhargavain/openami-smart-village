Section 01

The Dual Crisis: Energy Poverty + Mobility Poverty

The 789 million people still without electricity and the 1.2 billion in rural areas without reliable transport are not separate problems — they are the same population, experiencing compounding disadvantage. Solving energy without solving mobility leaves agricultural value chains broken. Solving mobility without clean energy perpetuates fuel dependence.

⚡
Energy Access Gap
  • 789M people have no electricity access (IEA 2023)
  • 570M are in Sub-Saharan Africa — 43% of SSA population
  • South Asia: 30M still unconnected (India/BD/Nepal/SL gap)
  • Grid extension: $2,000–5,000 per rural household; uneconomic beyond 5km
  • Diesel genset alternative: $0.40–0.80/kWh — 8× solar MiniGrid cost
  • MiniGrid solar: $0.05–0.12/kWh once operational — but requires load anchor
🛣️
Last-Mile Mobility Gap
  • 1 billion rural farmers lack reliable farm-to-market transport
  • Post-harvest losses: 20–40% due to transport failure (IFAD)
  • Petrol motorbike/tuk-tuk OPEX: $80–140/month in SSA
  • ICE three-wheeler in India: ₹8,000–12,000/month fuel cost
  • Rural roads: 60–70% unpaved — standard EVs fail in wet conditions
  • Women-led small businesses: disproportionately affected by transport cost
🔄
The MiniGrid Profitability Trap
  • MiniGrid load factor: often <25% (night-time household lighting only)
  • Surplus solar power wasted 8am–5pm — grid operators lose revenue
  • Low load factor = higher per-kWh tariff = reduced affordability = lower uptake
  • Chicken-and-egg: won't invest in MiniGrid without customers; no customers without grid
  • eATV charging as daytime anchor load solves this — 2–4 kWh/vehicle/day absorbed
  • Productive use anchors increase MiniGrid IRR from 8% to 18–24% (ESMAP studies)
💡
Why Now? The Convergence Moment
  • LiFePO4 cell cost: $80/kWh in 2024 — 85% reduction since 2012
  • Solar PV module cost: $0.18/W in 2024 — below $0.10/W by 2027
  • India EV policy: FAME-II, PM E-Drive — ₹10,900 crore deployment
  • Africa: AfDB $25B energy access commitment; ECREEE MiniGrid scale-up
  • Bangladesh: National EV Policy 2023 — target 30% EV penetration by 2030
  • Digital payment rails: M-PESA, UPI, bKash enabling PAYG models at village scale

SSA Competitive Moat: The Local Assembly Advantage
Kenya (Nairobi), Tanzania (Dar es Salaam), and Nigeria (Lagos/Ogun) all have nascent EV assembly ecosystems (BasiGo, Roam, Ampersand, Spiro). Our model targets assembly partnerships with these local players — importing semi-knocked-down (SKD) kits and assembling locally. This avoids import duties of 25–35% on fully-built vehicles, creates local jobs, enables community ownership co-operatives, and creates a servicing network essential for rural fleet uptime.

South Asia — Market Analysis & Expansion Strategy

South Asia presents a qualitatively different opportunity from SSA: larger industrial base, more advanced regulatory frameworks (especially India), higher population density, and a massive existing three-wheeler / cargo vehicle market ripe for electrification. The challenge is more about navigating regulatory complexity and competitive incumbents than building from scratch.

🇮🇳 India — Anchor Market
  • Off-grid population: 15–30M (remote Northeast, islands, tribal areas)
  • Rural three-wheeler market: 800,000 units/year — fastest-growing EV segment
  • FAME-II subsidy: ₹10,000–15,000 per EV (three-wheeler cargo)
  • PM E-DRIVE: ₹10,900 crore 2024 — ₹2,000 crore for e3W cargo
  • State subsidies: Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Karnataka — additional ₹30,000–50,000
  • MiniGrid policy: No dedicated MiniGrid policy — solar-diesel hybrid farms via RE programs
  • Target geography: Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh tribal districts; Northeast (Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya); Sundarbans; Lakshadweep
  • Key partners: EESL, RESCO operators, Husk Power, OMC Power, SELCO
  • Vehicle candidates: Mahindra Treo, Piaggio Ape E-City, Euler HiLoad — or TrikeXplor upgraded variant
🇧🇩 Bangladesh — High Priority
  • Off-grid: 30M (Chars, Haors, coastal islands — Chittagong Hill Tracts)
  • Existing EV culture: 3M+ battery-powered electric rickshaws (ERVs) already operating
  • Charging: 90% informal grid-charging — MiniGrid solar upgrade is urgent need
  • National EV Policy 2023: 30% EV target by 2030; import duty exemption for EV components
  • IDCOL MiniGrid: 227 solar MiniGrid sites operational (2024) — scaling to 500+
  • bKash penetration: 70M+ registered users — PAYG vehicle financing highly feasible
  • Key risk: Cyclone/flood terrain requires IP67+ vehicle sealing and high ground clearance
  • Opportunity: Replace diesel Nosimon/Leguna tractors on Chars with solar-charged eATVs — direct economic impact for 5M+ farmers
🇳🇵 Nepal — Mountainous Niche
  • Geography: 75% mountainous — requires high ground clearance (200mm+), four-wheel traction
  • Existing market: 55% EV penetration in new three-wheeler sales (2024) — highest in South Asia
  • Hydropower base: Abundant low-cost electricity (₹4–6/kWh) — no solar needed for urban
  • Rural gap: High-altitude villages (above 2,500m) — solar + battery storage essential
  • Fit: eATV specifically suited to steep tracks inaccessible to standard three-wheelers
  • Policy: NEA (Nepal Electricity Authority) supportive; import duty 10% on EV kits
🇱🇰 Sri Lanka — Emerging Market
  • Post-crisis recovery: 2022 fuel crisis accelerated EV adoption — 140% YoY increase
  • Off-grid: Northern and Eastern provinces — 8% without reliable grid
  • Agricultural belt: Dry Zone (Anuradhapura, Polonnaruwa) — paddy and vegetable logistics need
  • EV policy: Import tax exemption for EVs under Rs. 6M; CEFPF green financing available
  • MiniGrid: CEB rural electrification program — 150+ off-grid communities
  • Opportunity: Plug into existing SLSEA solar program — add eATV charging as productive-use layer

Bangladesh — The Char/Haor Opportunity: A Priority Case
Bangladesh's 100+ riverine char islands and haor wetlands house 5–8 million people who farm paddy, lentils, and mustard but lose 25–40% of their harvest due to transport failure at water-level fluctuation. Current diesel-powered Nosimon tractors (locally-built bamboo and metal vehicles) cost $0.80–1.20/km to operate. An IP67-sealed eATV charged from a 10 kWp floating solar MiniGrid (already pioneered by Grameen Shakti and IDCOL) operating at $0.12–0.15/km would generate $300–400/year net saving per farmer household — equivalent to 15–20% income increase. PAYG financing at $35/month (48-month term) is within reach for char farmers earning $150–250/month. Runner Automobiles (Bangladesh's largest EV manufacturer) and Walton Hi-Tech Industries are immediate local assembly partnership targets.

⚡ Battery Swap as MiniGrid V2G (Vehicle-to-Grid) Architecture

The swappable 48V/60Ah LiFePO4 pack (eATV Pro) is designed to double as a mobile energy storage unit. When the eATV is parked at the MiniGrid charging hub overnight, the battery pack connects via a bidirectional DC-DC interface, allowing the MiniGrid operator to use vehicle batteries as additional distributed storage during peak evening load (6pm–10pm). This V2G architecture increases effective BESS capacity by 40–80% without capital investment in fixed battery banks — a critical economic improvement for MiniGrid operators facing 4pm–8pm evening peak demand from households. Each eATV fleet of 5 vehicles adds 300 kWh of distributed storage per node. Fleet operators receive a grid-balancing payment from the MiniGrid operator (₹0.50–1.00/kWh discharged into grid) — creating an additional revenue stream.

Carbon Credit Uplift — Voluntary Carbon Market Revenue
Each eATV displacing a diesel tuk-tuk saves approximately 1.8–2.4 tCO₂e/year. At current voluntary carbon market prices ($8–15/tCO₂ for Verified Carbon Standard cookstove/clean energy projects), a fleet of 6 eATVs generates $86–216/year in carbon credits — not transformative but meaningful additional upside. At Scale (100 nodes = 600 vehicles), carbon revenue reaches $86,000–216,000/year. AFOLU (Agriculture, Forestry, Land Use) stacking methodology (post-harvest waste reduction → methane avoidance) can further uplift this. Verified by Gold Standard or Verra VCS for institutional buyers.
